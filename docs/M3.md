## What was built

- `quant/signals.py`: the 7 signals as known on each day, ranks, and the score.
- `quant/backtest.py`: data loading, a daily simulator and the variations.
- `quant/pit.py`: the point-in-time accessor (M3 start). `tests/test_signals.py` and `tests/test_pit.py` cover filing dates, the Q4 derivation, splits, scoring and a delisting exit.
- `.github/workflows/m3-backtest.yml`: a manual run of about 10 minutes. It reads the price years 2011 to 2019 only.
- **Experiments log:** every variation is written to R2 `inbox/experiments/`, and the nightly run adds them to the `experiments` table (the pipeline stays the only writer).
  - Three full runs so far, 58 variations each. All are logged, including the two whose results were later fixed (a share-count bug and a float-check bug).
  - The numbers below are from the final run, [40449355087](https://github.com/poojan-9603/StockWatch/actions/runs/40449355087).
- Tables in `docs/review/`:
  - [m3_backtest.csv](review/m3_backtest.csv): every variation.
  - [m3_ic.csv](review/m3_ic.csv): each signal's predictive power.
  - [m3_eligible.csv](review/m3_eligible.csv): how many stocks pass the buy rule.
  - The `m3_*` coverage, sector and price-issue tables.

How the backtest trades: decide at a rebalance day's close and trade at the next open; 0.1% cost per trade; S&P 500 members as of each day, including dead companies. Benchmarks: SPY (S&P 500 with dividends) and RSP (equal-weighted S&P 500).

## Decisions I made (recommended, yours to veto)

1. **Score definition: re-rank the equal-weighted mean 0 to 100 across the day's scored stocks.**
   - Read literally (the plain mean of 7 ranks must reach 80), the buy rule almost never fires: a median of 1 stock qualifies per week (0 to 4), and fewer than 10 qualify on all 408 days.
   - The result is about 7 positions, 34% cash and 7.6% a year.
   - Re-ranked, a median of 34 stocks qualify (18 to 47). "Score 80" then means the top 20%.
   - This changes how the plan's buy rule reads, so please confirm it.
2. **Signal definitions: quality = ROA, value = book/market.**
   - Picked for coverage (87% and 86%, the highest of the candidates) and as the standard definitions. Not picked on backtest results.
   - ROE and sales yield looked better in some runs, but their correlations are noise (t 1.08 and 0.11). Switching to them would be fitting the tuning years.
3. **Earnings surprise:**
   - The latest quarter's diluted EPS change from the same quarter a year earlier, divided by the spread of the last 8 such changes (at least 4).
   - Q4 is the fiscal year less the nine months. Missing if the quarter ended over 200 days ago.
   - Coverage 90%.
4. **Sectors: SIC mapped to the 11 GICS-style sectors.**
   - Smallest sector averages 20 members a month (Communication Services), median 43.
   - Today's members agree with Wikipedia's GICS sector 81.7% of the time.
   - The alternatives are lopsided: SIC divisions put 197 members in Manufacturing and 1 in Agriculture, and the SEC review office has groups of 1.
5. **Tag fallbacks:**
   - Revenue: the first of 5 revenue tags (91% coverage).
   - Gross profit: the tag, else revenue less cost (60%, against 38% from the tag alone).
   - Shares: cover-page count, else balance sheet (94%).
6. **Market value:**
   - Close × latest shares, adjusted for splits since the share count's date.
   - Treated as missing when the SEC public float contradicts it, using the identity check's test (float ÷ value on the float date outside 0.2 to 1.25).
   - Coverage 93% of member-days.
   - Known gap: Fox still comes out near $0 (a share-count problem the float check doesn't catch because there's no float figure to compare). It's one company.
   - Multi-class companies: SEC's companyfacts leaves out per-class counts, so no company showed several counts in one filing. Alphabet's value checks out ($925bn at the end of 2019). Berkshire gets no value signal.
7. **Price history for signals:**
   - Only validated stretches (from index entry or 2010).
   - A newly added stock has no momentum or volatility for its first year (94.7% of member-months have 13 months).
   - Missing counts as 50.
8. **Unverified vendor stretches count as priced.**
   - 298 member-months (0.7%) across 11 companies: PG&E 72, Fox 62, DISH 34, CSRA 28, PetSmart 26, others under 25.
   - Dropping them would remove PG&E's 2019 bankruptcy, which flatters the backtest.
9. **Unpriced stretches can't be bought; a holding whose prices stop exits at its last close.**
   - 398 member-months are unpriced (0.95%).
   - 175 price series end before 2019-12 (mostly acquisitions, plus WIKI's 2018 freeze).
   - Failed (30% penalty) means an 8-K item 1.03 (bankruptcy) within a year before the prices stop. Only 1 company qualifies, and the design never held it.
   - In the final run, 3 holdings exited this way, all as acquisitions.
10. **Quarantined days:** a stock can't be bought on one. 9 company-days in the tuning years.
11. **Dividend rule (approved in M1), applied to AT&T only.**
    - Our rebuilt yfinance dividends for AT&T are 1.324× what was paid, because Yahoo files the 2022 WarnerMedia spin-off as a "split". The backtest divides them back, and nothing is mixed in from another source.
    - Five other tuning-year stocks fail the SEC yearly dividend check: Progressive, Prologis, Visa, Booking and Nabors.
    - For Progressive, Prologis and Visa, the 30-stock comparison shows yfinance matching Tiingo (one date apart each), so those are fiscal-year timing in the SEC check, not bad data.
    - Booking and Nabors were not in the comparison and have only 2 checked years each. They're left as they are and flagged here.
12. **Rebalance: monthly.**
    - Monthly made 12.5% at 1.03 turnover against weekly's 12.3% at 1.12, within noise. Monthly means fewer trades.
    - The two-check sell confirmation then spans two months, not two weeks.
13. **Market filter: soft, as planned.**
    - "Bad mood" means SPY below its 200-day average with VIX above 20.
    - In bad mood, the buy bar rises 5 points and new positions are half size.
    - Results: soft 12.5% monthly and 12.3% weekly; none 12.5% and 11.8%; hard 8.6% and 8.3%.
    - The hard filter clearly hurts. Soft against none is a wash.
    - VIX comes from yfinance (^VIX) because FRED timed out four times from GitHub's runners.
14. **Dropped as not supported (the plan keeps them only if the backtest supports them):**

    | Feature | Monthly | Weekly |
    |---|---|---|
    | Timing layer | -1.0 points | +0.4 |
    | Score nudge on size | -2.1 | -0.6 |
    | Volatility stop | -0.7 | +0.8 |

15. **Sizing numbers (the plan gives no exact values):**
    - Base weight 1/15, scaled by the median volatility over the stock's own volatility.
    - Minimum 2%, cap 10% (1.5× the average), at most 20 positions, swap at 20+ points better.
    - **Knobs that didn't bind:** the buy bar (75/85) and sell level (35/45) changed nothing. With 34 stocks eligible, the top ones clear any of those bars, and the 20-point swap replaces a fading holding before its score drops below 40.

**Not in the backtest:** news (no history), the hidden-twin flag (it's a flag, not a rule), and the drawdown alarm (a review, not a trade). Sharpe uses a zero risk-free rate for both sides.

## Proposal: a fresh hold-out for the next design

You decided (2026-10-27) to keep the frozen design running on paper, carry on with M4 to M6, and rework the quant design separately. The 2020+ years are spent, so the next design needs somewhere new to be tested.

**The hard limit first.** No hold-out can prove a small edge quickly. The design's monthly excess return over SPY swings by about 7% a year (tuning) to 11% a year (test), worked back from the t-stats. To show an edge at t = 2 takes about (2 × 8% / edge)² years: about 10 years for a 5-point edge and about 28 for a 3-point edge. A hold-out can catch a bad design and build a track record. It can't certify a good one in months.

**Options, measured against what we have:**

| Option | Fresh? | Ready when | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reuse 2020+ | No, we've seen the result | Now | Ruled out (hard rule 1) |
| Before 2013 | Yes | Now | Ruled out: history starts in 2010, and signals need about 3 years of warm-up (8 quarters of EPS changes, 12 months of momentum), so nothing usable is left |
| Split the companies: design on half the S&P 500 (by a fixed hash of CIK), check on the other half, both 2013 to 2019 | Partly: different stocks, same years and same market swings | Now | Use it as a **pre-freeze check**: it catches a design fitted to particular stocks, not one fitted to the period |
| **Live forward data**: everything after the new design's freeze date | Fully: no one has seen it, and it's truly point in time (captured nightly) | Grows a day at a time | **The real hold-out** |

**Recommendation:**
1. Design on 2013 to 2019 using half A of the companies. Before freezing, check on half B. Freeze only if it holds on B. One look at B per frozen candidate, logged like everything else.
2. Freeze with a git tag and a date. The lock gains a second date: no tuning on anything after the freeze.
3. The live data after the freeze is the gate. Pre-register checkpoints now, matching the plan's kill rule: 12 months (review only), 24 months (must be winning, or no real money), 36 months (stop). The paper period (M7) still applies on top.
4. Meanwhile the current frozen design keeps running on paper as the baseline the new one has to beat.

**Live scoring, decided 2026-10-27:** paper mode means scoring today's data, which hard rule 1 used to forbid. You approved live scoring of the latest day for paper mode and the panel, with backtests, tuning and evaluation on 2020+ still forbidden. Hard rule 1 now says so. `quant.lock.live(day)` lets a scoring call read history up to `day` only when `day` is within 7 days of today, so no past day can be scored. Also approved: the scoreboard tracks the app's own recorded live verdicts against SPY from the paper start date. That is the forward hold-out, so it reads the verdict log and prices, and never re-scores a past day.
