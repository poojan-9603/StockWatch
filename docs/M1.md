# M1: Backfill plan

**Done when** (PLAN.md): quality checks pass on the full history, and the 30-stock source comparison has been reviewed.

Measured 2026-10-25 before writing any code. Scratch scripts, not in the repo.

## What the measurements say

### Membership history

**Primary source: [fja05680/sp500](https://github.com/fja05680/sp500).** MIT license, 957 stars, last updated 2026-09-07. It has 2,720 dated snapshots from 1996-01-02 to 2026-08-18. Since 2010, every snapshot has 496 to 507 members.

**Cross-check: Wikipedia, [Historical components of the S&P 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_components_of_the_S%26P_500).** It has 359 change rows since 2010 and is already current to 2026-10-06. (The changes table moved off the main "List of S&P 500 companies" page.) The other candidate, hanshof/sp500_constituents, stopped updating in August 2025.

| Since 2010 | Wikipedia | fja | Same date | Within 7 days | Wikipedia only | fja only |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Additions | 340 | 367 | 253 | 62 | 25 | 52 |
| Removals | 337 | 363 | 225 | 78 | 34 | 60 |

How to read that:

- **Most "fja only" rows are ticker renames, not membership changes.** fja records a rename as a same-day add plus remove. Examples: FI→FISV, ABC→COR, FLT→CPAY, MMC→MRSH, BK→BNY, PARA→PSKY. Wikipedia doesn't list renames. These can be detected automatically.
- **fja lags about two months.** Six changes from 2026-09-21 to 2026-10-06 are only in Wikipedia.
- **Unmatched rows inside the tuning years (2010 to 2019):** 18 + 28 Wikipedia-only and 28 + 37 fja-only, about 110 events. After the automatic rename detection, the rest need a by-hand review against a third source (S&P press releases or 8-Ks). Example: the FOX/FOXA dates in 2019. **This is the most manual part of M1.**

### Size of the backfill

- **852 unique tickers** were in the S&P 500 at some point since 2010. That counts a rename twice. 503 are members now, and 349 left.
- Of the 349 that left, Tiingo's list has 253 under the same ticker on an exchange and 5 only on OTC. **91 aren't found by ticker.** Spot checks show these are mostly renames (Tiingo only keeps the current ticker: BK is now BNY, FI is FISV, FB is META) plus failures under new OTC tickers (FRC is FRCB).
- **About 140 to 230 companies no longer trade** and need Tiingo instead of yfinance. That fits in one month of Tiingo's 500-symbol cap. At the free plan's 50 requests an hour, it's about 5 hours of throttled fetching, so the job checkpoints and resumes.
- **Every company needs a CIK** (PLAN rule 10). The CIK is the SEC's permanent company ID. Wikipedia's current list gives CIKs for the 503 members. The ~350 removed ones need matching by name against `submissions.zip` (current and former names), then a by-hand review of leftovers.

### Will the EDGAR bulk files fit?

- **Runner:** a private-repo `ubuntu-latest` runner has 2 CPUs, 8 GB RAM, and 14 GB SSD, with a 6-hour job limit.
- **Files:** `companyfacts.zip` (1.4 GB) plus `submissions.zip` (1.6 GB) is 3 GB. They're read one company at a time straight from the zip, never unpacked, so disk and memory are fine.
- **R2 free tier:** 10 GB storage, 1M Class A (write) operations, 10M Class B (read) operations a month, free egress. Keeping one raw copy of each zip uses about 3 GB of that.

Sources: [GitHub-hosted runners](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/reference/runners/github-hosted-runners), [Actions limits](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/reference/limits), [R2 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/)

## Build order

Riskiest first. Each step ends with something I can show you.

1. **yfinance from Actions smoke test.** Pull 20 tickers from a GitHub runner. If Yahoo blocks data-center IPs, the price plan changes, so we find out first.
2. **Membership spells.** Load fja, auto-detect renames, diff against Wikipedia, and write the disagreements to a CSV for review. You confirm or override the tuning-year rows. Output: `market/membership.parquet`.
3. **Ticker → CIK map with date ranges.** Sources:
   - SEC `company_tickers.json` for current tickers.
   - Wikipedia's CIK column.
   - `submissions.zip` name and former-name matching for dead companies.
   - A by-hand review of leftovers.

   This step also maps each dead company to its Tiingo ticker. Output: `market/ticker_map.parquet`.
4. **EDGAR backfill.**
   - Save the bulk zips to `raw/` in R2.
   - Stream-parse them into point-in-time facts with filed dates (`market/fundamentals/`).
   - Add SIC codes for sectors.
   - Add 8-K filings with item numbers, which the 40%-move check uses.
   - Rate-limited to under 10 requests a second, with a User-Agent that carries contact info.
5. **Price backfill.**
   - yfinance for live names, raw OHLCV plus splits and dividends.
   - Tiingo for dead names.
   - Raw responses go to R2 first, then get cleaned. Checkpointed, and idempotent on (CIK, date).
6. **Quality checks** from PLAN.md, run over the full history:
   - completeness against the market calendar
   - price sanity
   - 40% moves
   - staleness
   - the accounting identity (assets = liabilities + equity)
   - the point-in-time test

   Each failure is sorted into block, quarantine, or log.
7. **30-stock source comparison.** yfinance against Tiingo on 30 stocks, written up for your review.

Each step is its own commit, and each step is checkpointed and safe to rerun.

## Decisions (approved 2026-10-25)

1. **2020+ data:** may be downloaded and data-quality checked. It is never scored or evaluated (no signals, returns, scores, or strategy judgments).
2. **Membership:** fja is primary, with Wikipedia as the cross-check and for recent changes. Tuning-year disagreements get a by-hand review.
3. **Price depth:** 2010 onward for the whole live universe.
4. **Raw EDGAR zips:** only the latest copy is kept in R2.
5. **SEC User-Agent:** comes from the `SEC_USER_AGENT` repo secret. As of 2026-10-25 it isn't on `StockWatch` yet, and it's needed before step 3 (the first SEC download).

## Original decision list

1. **2020 and later data in M1.** Your rule bans backtests, scoring, and any evaluation on 2020+ data. M1 has to *download* 2020+ data (the live app needs it) and run *data-quality* checks on it ("pass on the full history"). My reading: download and data-quality checks are allowed, while signals, returns, scores, and anything that judges the strategy are not. **Please confirm.**
2. **Membership source:** fja as primary, Wikipedia for the cross-check and recent changes, with the ~110 tuning-year disagreements reviewed by hand and the result recorded in a file in the repo.
3. **Price history depth for the live universe beyond the S&P 500** (small and mid caps, a few thousand stocks):
   - (a) 2010 onward for everything. This is simplest, at about 300 MB of Parquet.
   - (b) 2 years, enough for the signals but nothing more.

   I'd pick (a).
4. **Raw EDGAR zips in R2:** keep only the latest copy (about 3 GB of the 10 GB free tier), overwritten on each backfill run.
5. **SEC contact for the User-Agent:** SEC asks for a name and an admin email. Please add a repo secret `SEC_USER_AGENT`, like `StockWatch yourname@example.com`, with whichever email you want SEC to see.
