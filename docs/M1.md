# M1: Backfill plan

**Done when** (PLAN.md): quality checks pass on the full history, and the 30-stock source comparison has been reviewed.

Measured 2026-10-25 before writing any code. Scratch scripts, not in the repo.

## What the measurements say

### Membership history

**Primary source: [fja05680/sp500](https://github.com/fja05680/sp500).** MIT license, 957 stars, last updated 2026-09-07. It has 2,720 dated snapshots from 1996-01-02 to 2026-08-18. Since 2010, every snapshot has 496 to 507 members.

**Cross-check: Wikipedia, [Historical components of the S&P 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_components_of_the_S%26P_500).** It has 359 change rows since 2010 and is already current to 2026-10-06. (The changes table moved off the main "List of S&P 500 companies" page.) The other candidate, hanshof/sp500_constituents, stopped updating in August 2025.

| Since 2010 | Wikipedia | fja | Same date | Within 7 days | Wikipedia only | fja only |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Additions | 340 | 367 | 253 | 62 | 25 | 52 |
| Removals | 337 | 363 | 225 | 78 | 34 | 60 |

How to read that:

- **Most "fja only" rows are ticker renames, not membership changes.** fja records a rename as a same-day add plus remove. Examples: FI→FISV, ABC→COR, FLT→CPAY, MMC→MRSH, BK→BNY, PARA→PSKY. Wikipedia doesn't list renames. These can be detected automatically.
- **fja lags about two months.** Six changes from 2026-09-21 to 2026-10-06 are only in Wikipedia.
- **Unmatched rows inside the tuning years (2010 to 2019):** 18 + 28 Wikipedia-only and 28 + 37 fja-only, about 110 events. After the automatic rename detection, the rest need a by-hand review against a third source (S&P press releases or 8-Ks). Example: the FOX/FOXA dates in 2019. **This is the most manual part of M1.**

### Size of the backfill

- **852 unique tickers** were in the S&P 500 at some point since 2010. That counts a rename twice. 503 are members now, and 349 left.
- Of the 349 that left, Tiingo's list has 253 under the same ticker on an exchange and 5 only on OTC. **91 aren't found by ticker.** Spot checks show these are mostly renames (Tiingo only keeps the current ticker: BK is now BNY, FI is FISV, FB is META) plus failures under new OTC tickers (FRC is FRCB).
- **About 140 to 230 companies no longer trade** and need Tiingo instead of yfinance. That fits in one month of Tiingo's 500-symbol cap. At the free plan's 50 requests an hour, it's about 5 hours of throttled fetching, so the job checkpoints and resumes.
- **Every company needs a CIK** (PLAN rule 10). The CIK is the SEC's permanent company ID. Wikipedia's current list gives CIKs for the 503 members. The ~350 removed ones need matching by name against `submissions.zip` (current and former names), then a by-hand review of leftovers.

### Will the EDGAR bulk files fit?

- **Runner:** a private-repo `ubuntu-latest` runner has 2 CPUs, 8 GB RAM, and 14 GB SSD, with a 6-hour job limit.
- **Files:** `companyfacts.zip` (1.4 GB) plus `submissions.zip` (1.6 GB) is 3 GB. They're read one company at a time straight from the zip, never unpacked, so disk and memory are fine.
- **R2 free tier:** 10 GB storage, 1M Class A (write) operations, 10M Class B (read) operations a month, free egress. Keeping one raw copy of each zip uses about 3 GB of that.

Sources: [GitHub-hosted runners](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/reference/runners/github-hosted-runners), [Actions limits](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/reference/limits), [R2 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/)

## Build order

Riskiest first. Each step ends with something I can show you.

1. **yfinance from Actions smoke test.** Pull 20 tickers from a GitHub runner. If Yahoo blocks data-center IPs, the price plan changes, so we find out first.
2. **Membership spells.** Load fja, auto-detect renames, diff against Wikipedia, and write the disagreements to a CSV for review. You confirm or override the tuning-year rows. Output: `market/membership.parquet`.
3. **Ticker → CIK map with date ranges.** Sources:
   - SEC `company_tickers.json` for current tickers.
   - Wikipedia's CIK column.
   - `submissions.zip` name and former-name matching for dead companies.
   - A by-hand review of leftovers.

   This step also maps each dead company to its Tiingo ticker. Output: `market/ticker_map.parquet`.
4. **EDGAR backfill.**
   - Save the bulk zips to `raw/` in R2.
   - Stream-parse them into point-in-time facts with filed dates (`market/fundamentals/`).
   - Add SIC codes for sectors.
   - Add 8-K filings with item numbers, which the 40%-move check uses.
   - Rate-limited to under 10 requests a second, with a User-Agent that carries contact info.
5. **Price backfill.**
   - yfinance for live names, raw OHLCV plus splits and dividends.
   - Tiingo for dead names.
   - Raw responses go to R2 first, then get cleaned. Checkpointed, and idempotent on (CIK, date).
6. **Quality checks** from PLAN.md, run over the full history:
   - completeness against the market calendar
   - price sanity
   - 40% moves
   - staleness
   - the accounting identity (assets = liabilities + equity)
   - the point-in-time test

   Each failure is sorted into block, quarantine, or log.
7. **30-stock source comparison.** yfinance against Tiingo on 30 stocks, written up for your review.

Each step is its own commit, and each step is checkpointed and safe to rerun.

## Decisions (approved 2026-10-25)

1. **2020+ data:** may be downloaded and data-quality checked. It is never scored or evaluated (no signals, returns, scores, or strategy judgments).
2. **Membership:** fja is primary, with Wikipedia as the cross-check and for recent changes. Tuning-year disagreements get a by-hand review.
3. **Price depth:** 2010 onward for the whole live universe.
4. **Raw EDGAR zips:** only the latest copy is kept in R2.
5. **SEC User-Agent:** comes from the `SEC_USER_AGENT` repo secret. As of 2026-10-25 it isn't on `StockWatch` yet, and it's needed before step 3 (the first SEC download).

## Step 1 result: yfinance from Actions (2026-10-25)

Run [40237393622](https://github.com/poojan-9603/StockWatch/actions/runs/40237393622), workflow `yfinance-check`, yfinance 1.7.0. I ran the same script from this machine first, and the results were identical.

**Yahoo serves GitHub runners: PASS at this scale.**

- 19 of 20 tickers came back. The 20th, TWTR, is dead and came back empty, as expected; dead names go to Tiingo.
- Most tickers have 4,228 rows from 2010-01-04 to 2026-10-23. Later listings start at their IPO (META 2012, TSLA 2010-06-29, ELF 2016).
- Single calls took 5 s in total. The 19-ticker batch call took 2 s.
- **Not tested:** behavior at a few thousand tickers. Step 5 will add a rate limiter and measure.

**FAIL against plan rule 9 ("store raw prices"):** yfinance's `Close` is **split-adjusted**, not raw, even with `auto_adjust=False`. Only dividends are left unadjusted.

- NVDA on 2024-06-07 shows 120.89. The real close that day, before the 10-for-1 split, was about 1,208.
- Yahoo also rewrites every past row when a new split happens. So yesterday's saved "close" and today's can disagree for the same date.

Options:

- **(a) Rebuild raw prices from yfinance** (recommended). Multiply each row's prices by the split ratios after that date, using yfinance's own split table, and divide volume by them. It's a few lines, it gives stable raw history, and rule 9 holds as written. The step 7 comparison against Tiingo, which gives true raw closes, then checks the rebuild on 30 stocks.
- **(b) Use Tiingo for all raw history.** The 500-symbol monthly cap means about 3,000+ names take 7 or more months. Too slow.
- **(c) Store yfinance's split-adjusted prices as they are** and change rule 9. Simpler, but stored history would shift whenever a stock splits.

Two smaller findings, for later steps:

- **PSKY (Paramount Skydance) returned only 16 rows** (2026-07-29 to 2026-10-16) with 4 nulls. It should have about 300 rows. Recent corporate events seem to confuse Yahoo's ticker mapping. Step 5 needs a per-company row-count check against the market calendar, and anything this short gets quarantined.
- **FISV has 4,227 rows** where its peers have 4,228, so one day is missing. The completeness check will name the date.

**Decision (2026-10-25):** option (a). Raw prices get rebuilt from yfinance using its split table, rule 9 stays as written, and the step 7 Tiingo comparison validates the result.

## Step 2 result: membership spells by ticker (2026-10-25)

`pipeline/membership.py` does five things:

- It downloads fja's snapshots plus two Wikipedia pages (the changes table and the current list) and saves them raw.
- It turns the snapshots into spells, meaning periods of membership, from start date up to (but not including) end date, for each ticker.
- It applies Wikipedia changes dated after fja's last snapshot.
- It checks that today's members match Wikipedia's current list.
- It fails loudly on anything odd: snapshots out of order, a snapshot outside 480 to 520 members, unreadable dates, or a removal with no open spell.

`tests/test_membership.py` covers spells, re-entry, extension, and Wikipedia cell cleanup. All 4 tests pass.

**Real run:** 1,267 spells. fja's last snapshot is 2026-08-18, and the 6 Wikipedia changes after it were applied. 503 current members. **The current-list check fails on one name:** we have PSKY, Wikipedia has SKYD. Paramount Skydance changed ticker (yfinance has full SKYD history, and Tiingo lists SKYD from 2025-08-06). Neither source records renames as events. This also explains the 16-row PSKY result in step 1.

**Finding that changes the order of work:** after the automatic matching, about 95 fja/Wikipedia disagreements since 2010 remain. Most are the same event spelled with different tickers. fja often uses a later or OTC ticker, while Wikipedia uses the ticker at the time:

| fja | Wikipedia |
|---|---|
| JOY | JOYG |
| ANRZQ | ANR |
| EKDKQ | EK |
| AABA | YHOO |
| LDOS | SAI |
| KDP | DPS |
| VIAV | JDSU |
| DXC | CSC |

The same-day "rename" pattern also catches real changes: BNI → BRK.B in 2010 was Berkshire buying BNSF. So the fja/Wikipedia reconciliation, and the current-list check, have to compare **companies (CIK)**, not tickers. The by-hand review list comes after step 3 (ticker → CIK map). It will be much shorter than 95, since ticker spellings collapse onto the same CIK.

## Step 3 status: ticker → CIK map (in progress, 2026-10-25)

**SEC download works.** Run [40240078348](https://github.com/poojan-9603/StockWatch/actions/runs/40240078348) used `SEC_USER_AGENT`.

**Matching** (`pipeline/ticker_map.py`, not yet in the workflow): 871 membership spells overlap 2010 onward.

| Result | Spells |
|---|---|
| Wikipedia current list (CIK given) | 487 |
| Company name from a Wikipedia change row, matched to SEC names | 274 |
| Name borrowed from the Wikipedia row for the same event (fja ticker spelled differently) | 10 |
| Rename backed by an SEC name change | 6 |
| Current SEC ticker (open spells only) | 1 |
| **Needs review** ([docs/review/ticker_map_unresolved.csv](review/ticker_map_unresolved.csv)) | **93**, of which 86 overlap the tuning years |

**This is more complex than the plan assumed, for three reasons:**

1. **Tickers get reused.** PARA now belongs to Banzai International, a former SPAC, not Paramount. An early version matched by current SEC ticker and silently gave Paramount's 2022 to 2025 membership to Banzai. Current tickers are now trusted only for spells that are still open.
2. **A same-day ticker swap in fja isn't always a rename.**
   - Some are acquisitions: BNI → BRK.B (Berkshire bought BNSF), BDK → OKE, and ACS → URBN.
   - MYL → VTRS was a merger that formed a new company with its own CIK.
   - Only swaps where SEC shows a matching name change are accepted automatically (Anthem → Elevance, Symantec → NortonLifeLock → Gen, and 4 more).
3. **One ticker can span two SEC companies (19 cases).** Reorganizations and re-domiciles create a new CIK under the same ticker.
   - GOOGL was Google Inc. until 2015-10-02, then Alphabet.
   - Same pattern: Eaton 2012, ICE 2013, Medtronic 2014, Walgreens 2014, Broadcom 2018, Cigna 2018, Disney 2019, Fox 2019, APA 2021, Bunge 2023, BlackRock 2024, Exxon 2026.
   - For point-in-time fundamentals, the earlier years must use the old CIK's filings. So `ticker_map` needs **date-ranged CIK segments inside one membership spell**. PLAN rule 10 calls for this; the current code maps a whole spell to one CIK.

**Proposed fix (needs your OK):**

- `ticker_map` rows become (ticker, cik, from, to), so one spell can have several CIK segments.
- For the 19 two-company cases, find the predecessor automatically: a company with the same normalized name (current or former) that stopped filing within a year of the successor's first filing, like Eaton Corp → Eaton Corp plc. If the names differ (Google → Alphabet), the case goes to review.
- For the rest of the review list (about 70 spells), I check each one against SEC names and filing dates. Each answer goes into `pipeline/ticker_cik_overrides.csv` with a one-line evidence note, and you spot-check the file.
- Then the fja against Wikipedia reconciliation by company, as planned.
