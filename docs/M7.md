# M7: the paper period

**Done when** (PLAN.md): 2 to 3 months live on paper, with no broken-app failures in the last month.

**Earliest finish:** the first paper trades fill at the open on Monday, Nov 2, 2026. Two months later is Jan 4, 2027, and the 30 days before that must be clean. Every failure pushes the finish line out.

## Built (2026-10-29)

- **Broken-app count** (`monitor/failures.py`), every night, from GitHub's own run history:
  - a scheduled run of nightly, watch, weekly, restore or prices that failed or timed out (a bad-data block fails the nightly, so it counts)
  - a trading day with no successful nightly at all (GitHub dropped the run, or every try failed)
  - LLM spend over the $10 cap
  - Shown on Health as a check, and on the monthly report as a note. If GitHub's history can't be read, Health says "not measured tonight".
- **Scoreboard numbers** on the monthly report, from the paper replay:
  - turnover and trading costs since the start
  - each slice's return (S&P 500 members, everything else) against the S&P 500
  - a luck note: how far ahead or behind the S&P 500, and whether that's still within luck
  - "winning" by the plan's definition (more return, Sharpe at least equal, worst drop at most 1.5 times as deep)
- **Restore test on Health:** the monthly restore test now records its date and result, and Health shows it.
- **Already in place:** the universe rebuilds monthly (the prices job), the restore test runs on the first Saturday, and the emergency brake shows on the monthly report.

## Decisions I made (yours to veto)

1. **What counts as broken:**
   - **Counts:** failed scheduled runs and missing nights.
   - **Doesn't count:** cancelled runs (the shared R2 lock cancels a queued run when a newer one arrives) and manual runs (those are fixes).
   - A failed scheduled run counts even if I rescue the night by hand.
2. **The count starts at the paper start (Oct 30).** Before that, the nightly didn't exist yet.
3. **"Winning" stays "too early" until 12 months in,** the plan's first review. Two or three months can't say anything about it.
4. **Luck:** the t-statistic of daily returns over the S&P 500's. A t of about 2 either way is needed before a gap means anything.
5. **Slice returns** compound from what was held at the previous close. A stock bought or sold at an open is counted close to close that day. The error is small at monthly trading.

## Needs your decision

1. **When does the panel count as right?**
   - **Why it matters:** the plan gives the panel a vote only if it's right clearly more often across 30 disagreements. It doesn't say what "right" means, and the rule must be set before any results.
   - **My recommendation:**
     - A disagreement is settled 3 months after the panel's view.
     - The side whose call matched the stock's return against the S&P 500 wins.
     - "Clearly more often" means a one-sided binomial test at p < 0.05, counted only once 30 disagreements are settled.
   - **Until you decide:** the monthly report says the panel's record is waiting.
2. **GitHub is dropping most scheduled runs.**
   - **Watch:** on Oct 29 (UTC) it was scheduled for 12 hourly slots in market hours and ran in 2 (18:27 and 23:16). Three more runs came at 02:25, 05:49 and 08:53, in the night. Those were late leftovers from earlier slots, and they fell in quiet hours.
   - **Nightly:** the Oct 29 nightly started at 07:02 instead of 01:17.
   - This is one day of data, but GitHub documents that scheduled runs can be delayed or dropped under load.
   - **Effect:** a missing nightly counts as broken, so if this continues, M7 can't pass. The watch isn't hourly as the plan says, and an urgent 8-K could wait hours.
   - **Fix (a plan change, so I haven't built it):** a free Cloudflare cron trigger that starts the GitHub workflows on time through GitHub's API. That needs a narrow GitHub token stored in Cloudflare. I'd measure a week first, then decide.
