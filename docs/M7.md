**Done when** (PLAN.md): 2 to 3 months live on paper, with no broken-app failures in the last month.

**Earliest finish:** the first paper trades fill at the open on Monday, Nov 2, 2026. Two months later is Jan 4, 2027, and the 30 days before that must be clean. Every failure pushes the finish line out.

## Built (2026-10-29)

- **Broken-app count** (`monitor/failures.py`), every night, from GitHub's own run history:
  - a scheduled run of nightly, watch, weekly, restore or prices that failed or timed out (a bad-data block fails the nightly, so it counts)
  - a trading day with no successful nightly at all (GitHub dropped the run, or every try failed)
  - LLM spend over the $10 cap
  - Shown on Health as a check, and on the monthly report as a note. If GitHub's history can't be read, Health says "not measured tonight".
- **Scoreboard numbers** on the monthly report, from the paper replay:
  - turnover and trading costs since the start
  - each slice's return (S&P 500 members, everything else) against the S&P 500
  - a luck note: how far ahead or behind the S&P 500, and whether that's still within luck
  - "winning" by the plan's definition (more return, Sharpe at least equal, worst drop at most 1.5 times as deep)
- **Restore test on Health:** the monthly restore test now records its date and result, and Health shows it.
- **Already in place:** the universe rebuilds monthly (the prices job), the restore test runs on the first Saturday, and the emergency brake shows on the monthly report.

## Decisions I made (yours to veto)

1. **What counts as broken:**
   - **Counts:** failed scheduled runs and missing nights.
   - **Doesn't count:** cancelled runs (the shared R2 lock cancels a queued run when a newer one arrives) and manual runs (those are fixes).
   - A failed scheduled run counts even if I rescue the night by hand.
2. **The count starts at the paper start (Oct 30).** Before that, the nightly didn't exist yet.
3. **"Winning" stays "too early" until 12 months in,** the plan's first review. Two or three months can't say anything about it.
4. **Luck:** the t-statistic of daily returns over the S&P 500's. A t of about 2 either way is needed before a gap means anything.
5. **Slice returns** compound from what was held at the previous close. A stock bought or sold at an open is counted close to close that day. The error is small at monthly trading.

## The panel's record (rule set 2026-10-29, before any results)

The plan gives the panel a vote only if it's right clearly more often across at least 30 disagreements. You approved this rule (`quant.live.panel_record`, run every night):

- **A call:** Buy, Add and Hold expect the stock to beat the S&P 500. Don't buy, Trim and Sell expect it to lag. Wait and "not enough information" make no call.
- **A disagreement:** the panel's view and the math verdict it was shown against make opposite calls.
- **Settled 3 months after the view:** the side whose call matched the stock's total return against SPY's was right.
- **The vote:** at least 30 settled, and a one-sided binomial test at p < 0.05.

Two details I added, yours to veto:

1. **One disagreement per stock at a time.** The panel reruns every week, so a stock it disagrees on for 3 months would otherwise count about 12 times, for what is really one call. Later views of that stock don't count until the first one settles.
2. **"Agrees" now means the same call, not the same word.** Before, a stock the math bought and the panel said to hold showed as a disagreement. The site and the record now use the same definition.

Each panel row now stores the math verdict it was compared with, so the record never has to be reconstructed. The first weekly run (Nov 1) is the first with it. This is the scoreboard exception in hard rule 1: it reads the app's own recorded views and prices, and never re-scores a past day.

## GitHub dropped most scheduled runs: Cloudflare starts them now (you approved 2026-10-30)

- **What I measured:** on Oct 29 (UTC) the watch was due in 12 market-hour slots and ran in 2 (18:27 and 23:16), plus late leftovers at 02:25, 05:49 and 08:53. The nightly started at 07:02 instead of 01:17. A missing nightly counts as broken, so M7 couldn't pass like this.
- **The fix:** `worker/src/scheduler.ts`, a Cloudflare Worker with one cron at :17 every hour. It works out which jobs are due (the same times as the old GitHub crons, tested hour by hour over a week) and starts them through GitHub's API with `by=cron`. The jobs keep their own gates (watch hours, first weekend, trading days).
- **Counting:** a run it starts is named "<job> (scheduled)" and counts exactly like a GitHub scheduled run. Runs you start by hand still don't count.
- **Its token:** a fine-grained GitHub token for this one repository with Actions read and write. It can start, cancel or rerun runs and nothing else: no code, no secrets. It lives in GitHub secrets (`DISPATCH_TOKEN`) and is copied into Cloudflare's secret store by `scheduler-deploy.yml`.
- **If it fails:** it tries 3 times per job, then the cron run is marked failed in Cloudflare's log, and the missed night shows on Health. Ceiling: if the token expires, nothing runs until it's replaced, so set a long expiry and I'll remind you before it lapses.
- **GitHub's own schedules stay for now** as a backup. Once the scheduler's first nightly and watch runs succeed, I remove them: duplicate runs are safe, but they waste minutes and a late one can bump a queued run.
