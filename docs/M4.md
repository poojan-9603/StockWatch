## Test set (built 2026-10-27)

- 98 8-Ks filed 2013 to 2019, 14 for each of 7 item types, picked with a fixed seed. Saved to R2 `agents/testset.jsonl` by the `agents-testset` workflow ([run 40589157029](https://github.com/poojan-9603/StockWatch/actions/runs/40589157029)).
- **Label leak (open question 1 from M3), decided: mask the headings.** Each 8-K names its own item and SEC title ("Item 2.02 Results of Operations and Financial Condition"). The builder replaces both with "[heading removed]".
  - Before masking, all 98 of 98 showed their label's item number or a key phrase.
  - After masking, 17 do. None of those 17 are SEC headings; they're the body describing the event:
    - 14 of 14 bankruptcy filings mention bankruptcy or receivership
    - one 2.02 filing has a risk factor about "results of operations"
    - two 5.02 filings have company-written subheadings like "Appointment of New Chief Executive Officer"
  - So bankruptcy is an easy label, and that's fair: it's the text the model is supposed to read. Labeling exhibits instead would have meant reading press releases, which are longer and still name the event.
- Size: 480k characters in total (median 3,700 per document), roughly 120k tokens for one pass over the set.

## Model comparison result (2026-10-28)

[Run 40774885526](https://github.com/poojan-9603/StockWatch/actions/runs/40774885526): 588 answers, none failed, $1.88 in total. Table in `docs/review/m4_models.csv`.

| | Correct | Same answer both runs | Weakest label | Cost per filing (batch) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Haiku 5.5 | 87.8% | 100% | other events, 71% | $0.0002 |
| Sonnet 5.5 | 89.8% | 99.0% | results of operations, 79% | $0.0032 |
| Opus 5.5 | 95.9% | 99.0% | material agreement, 93% | $0.0063 |

- **Every quote checked out.** All 588 quotes were found word for word, and no model said "not enough information". The differences are all in which label was picked.
- **Is the gap real?** Comparing the same filings (exact McNemar test):
  - Opus vs Haiku: Opus was right 9 times where Haiku was wrong, and the reverse happened once (p = 0.02). Unlikely to be luck.
  - Opus vs Sonnet: 7 vs 1 (p = 0.07). Borderline.
  - Sonnet vs Haiku: 2 vs 0. No real difference.
- **Many of Haiku's misses are defensible readings, not reading failures.** For example:
  - an 8-K filed as "other events" that announces a signed definitive agreement (Haiku said material agreement)
  - one that reports buying four buildings (Haiku said acquisition completed)
  - executive pay plans filed under 5.02 (Haiku said material agreement)

  The label is the item the company chose, so the test partly measures knowing SEC filing habits. Opus knows those habits better.
- **The choice rule, as fixed beforehand:**
  - Opus is the best (95.9%). Haiku is 8.1 points behind and Sonnet 6.1, so only Opus is within 5 points.
  - Opus is also consistent (99%), and its weakest label is 93%.
  - So the rule picks **Opus 5.5 for both routine and judge**.
- **That conflicts with the plan,** which names Haiku 5.5 for routine jobs, and possibly with the $10 cap. An unbatched Opus read of a short filing costs about $0.013, against about $0.0003 for Haiku. That's roughly 750 Opus reads a month at the cap, against 30,000 or more with Haiku.

**Decision (yours, 2026-10-28): Haiku 5.5 for routine reading, Opus 5.5 as judge and for catalyst reads.** This keeps the plan and knowingly overrides rule 1. The reasons: the cost (about 40 times cheaper per read, which leaves room under the $10 cap), and the fact that Haiku's misses were mostly filing-habit calls rather than misreadings. The judge has the final word on the panel's view, so it gets the most accurate model. Opus 5.5 lists at $4 / $20 per million input / output tokens (half that through the Batch API), which settles the plan's "stronger model's price" check.

**Watch for:** Haiku's weakest labels were "other events" (71%) and "results of operations" (79%). Live tracking of the panel should report how often the judge overrules the routine readers, so this choice gets re-checked on real use.

## Model comparison (how it works)

`agents.compare`, run by the manual `agents-compare` workflow:
- **Candidates:** Claude Haiku 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5, all at low effort (it's a classification), with no tools.
- **What each one does:** reads every test 8-K (cleaned and labeled as data, as the panel will see it) and picks one of the 7 events or "Not enough information". It also copies one sentence that shows the event.
- **What counts as correct:** the right label AND code finding the quote word for word in the document.
- **Consistency:** each document runs twice, and the share of documents with the same label both times is reported.
- **Also reported:** the "not enough information" rate, cost per document, and the weakest label.
- **Cost:** sent through the Message Batches API at half price (you asked to cut the cost; batching keeps both runs for every model, so consistency stays comparable). On the real test set:
  - likely about $2 in total (Haiku $0.03, Sonnet $0.67, Opus $1.34)
  - worst case $5.61, if every answer used its full 1,500-token allowance
  - before sending anything, it checks the worst case against a $6 budget and refuses if it's over
  - batches usually finish within an hour; after 5 hours it cancels them and fails loudly
  - an answer cut off at the token limit counts as wrong and shows in the "failed" column
- **No refusal fallback:** a declined answer counts as wrong rather than being quietly answered by another model, so every score belongs to the model named.
- **Recording:** results go to `review/m4_models.csv` and the experiments table.

**Choice rule, fixed before any results:**
1. **Routine model:** the cheapest candidate whose correct rate is within 5 points of the best, with at least 90% consistency and no label below 50% correct.
2. **Judge and catalyst reads:** the candidate with the highest correct rate (the cheaper one on a tie). It can be the same model as the routine one.
3. If no candidate meets rule 1, nothing is chosen. I bring you the numbers instead.

**Limit:** this tests reading and evidence on short filings, not the panel's bull, bear and judge reasoning. Those get tracked live once the panel runs.
